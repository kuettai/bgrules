⚙️ Quick Setup
  1. Place the Great Tunnel hex in the center of the table.
  2. Each player takes a Nest Mat and cubes in their color.
  3. Each player draws 1 random starting hex (gold border), places it connected to the Great Tunnel.
  4. Each player places 1 ant from supply into the Great Tunnel.
  5. Each player starts with: 5 larvae in chamber, 2 food in stores, 2 cards in hand.
  6. Separate centipede-icon cards → set aside (used for centipede battles & solo mode).
  7. Shuffle remaining cards into the player deck.
  8. Shuffle hex tiles into a face-down stack.
  9. Place round marker on Round 1 (Spring).
  10. Randomly determine start player → give Active Player Marker.
⚠️ 2-player setup is different: use reverse Great Tunnel, each player gets 2 random starting hexes (choose 1), start with 3 ants in placed hex, 3 larvae, 2 food, 2 cards.
💡 Solo setup: 1 starting hex with 3 ants, 3 larvae, 2 food, 4 cards. Create separate Centipede Deck from centipede-icon cards.
⚠️ Gotchas & Common Mistakes
  1. Play Card costs larvae/ants, NOT food. Every other action costs 1 food, but playing a card is paid with larvae or ants as shown on the card.
  2. Rest is free but restricted. You cannot rest as your first action of the round unless you have no cards AND no food.
  3. Reactions are free. Other players don't pay food for reactions — they happen automatically after the active player's action.
  4. Only the topmost evolution is active. When you stack a new evolution on a body segment, the old ability is covered. But caste bonuses (+1 per card in column) are cumulative.
  5. Defender wins ties in battle. The player who controls (inhabits) a collection site in the hex is the defender.
  6. Winner takes casualties too! Winner loses ants equal to half the loser's strength (rounded down). Don't forget this.
  7. Contested ≠ Shared. A hex is only contested if 2+ players have ants in the outskirts. If everyone is on collection sites, it's shared — no battle.
  8. Great Tunnel is safe. No battles ever happen in the Great Tunnel. It's never contested.
  9. Unfed ants eat larvae first. If you can't feed ants with food, each unfed ant consumes 1 larva. Only if no larvae remain are ants destroyed.
  10. Royal Decree skipped in final round. In Round 4 (or Rounds 3–4 in Long Game), there is no Royal Decree.
  11. March moves larvae first. Larvae move from chamber to an inhabited hex, then ants move between connected hexes. Order matters.
  12. Wormholes connect to ALL wormholes. An ant can move between any two wormhole hexes in a single step — they don't need to be adjacent.
  13. Colony Goals score EACH Slumber. They're not one-time — if you meet the condition every round, you score every round.
  14. Complete ant body = immediate 3 CP. As soon as you have at least one card in Head + Thorax + Abdomen, score 3 CP right then. You don't score again for the same body.
👷 Worker Phase — Detailed

Players take turns clockwise. The active player performs 1 action, then each other player may perform a Reaction (free). Play continues until 2 players have rested (3 in 5p).

🔍 Explore (1 food)

  1. Draw the top hex tile from the stack.
  2. Place it adjacent to any existing hex — tunnels must connect.
  3. Place 1 ant from your supply into the new hex.

Reaction: Each other player may place 1 larva from their chamber into any hex they already inhabit.

💡 Exploring is how you grow the map and claim territory early. The ant you place can immediately inhabit a collection site on the new hex.

🚶 March (1 food)

  1. Larvae first: Move larvae from your chamber to any hex you inhabit (these count as steps).
  2. Then ants: Move ants between adjacent hexes connected by tunnels.
  3. Total movement: up to 5 steps + 1 per thorax evolution card.
  4. Each ant/larva moved 1 hex = 1 step. You can split steps among multiple units.

Reaction: Each other player may move 1 ant 1 step.

⚠️ Larvae can only move FROM the chamber TO a hex you inhabit. They cannot move hex-to-hex. Ants move between connected hexes only (unless you have Tunneling or Flying Thorax).

🃏 Forage (1 food)

Draw 2 cards from the player deck.

Reaction: Each other player gains 1 larva (placed in their chamber).

🎴 Play a Card (costs larvae/ants — NOT food)

Play 1 card from your hand. Pay its cost in larvae and/or ants as shown on the card.

  • Event: One-time effect, then discarded.
  • Evolution: Placed on a body segment (Head/Thorax/Abdomen) of your Nest Mat. Stacks on top of existing evolutions.
  • Colony Goal: Placed face-up near your Nest Mat. Max 2 active goals at a time.

Reaction: Each other player may discard 1 card to draw 1 card.

💤 Rest (free)

  • First rest of the round: Gain 1 larva.
  • Each subsequent rest: Gain 1 food.
  • You cannot rest as your first action unless you have no cards AND no food.
  • Worker Phase ends when 2 players have rested (3 in 5p game).
  • The 2nd player to rest becomes the next start player.
⚠️ In a 2-player game: after one player rests, the other gets one final turn (any action, including rest), then the Worker Phase ends. That player keeps the Active Player Marker.
💡 Resting early gives you a larva but ends the phase sooner. Resting late lets you take more actions but your opponents may force the phase to end.
⚔️ Soldier Phase — Detailed

Resolve battles in all contested hexes. A hex is contested when it has ants from 2+ players AND at least one player has ants in the outskirts (not on a collection site).

Battle Resolution (5 Stages)

1. Army Strength
Count your ants in the hex + your Head caste bonus (+1 per Head evolution card in your stack).
2. Ferocity
Each player in the battle may secretly choose 1 card from hand to discard. All chosen cards are revealed simultaneously. Add the ferocity value (number in top-left of card) to your Army Strength.
3. Outcome
Highest total wins. Defender wins ties. (Defender = player who controls/inhabits a collection site in that hex.)
4. Casualties
Loser loses ants equal to the winner's total strength.
Winner loses ants equal to half the loser's total strength (rounded down).
Lone Survivor Rule: If casualties would destroy all ants on both sides, the winner keeps 1 ant.
5. Spoils
Winner gains 1 CP. Winner may move surviving ants to inhabit any empty collection sites in the hex.

🐛 Centipede Battles

When ants share a hex with a centipede token, battle occurs.

  • Centipede flips 2 cards from the centipede deck for its Army Strength (uses ferocity values).
  • If ants win: Destroy the centipede token. Gain 1 food + 1 CP.
  • If centipede wins: Lose ants equal to the centipede's full strength (not halved).
⚠️ Centipede casualties are NOT halved — you lose ants equal to the centipede's full strength if you lose. High risk, high reward.

Multi-Player Hex Battles

If 3+ players are in a contested hex, resolve battles pairwise. The defender (site controller) fights each attacker. Multiple battles may occur in the same hex.

👑 Queen Phase — Detailed

Step 1: Harvest

Collect resources from all collection sites you inhabit across the entire Meadow. Each site shows its resource: food, larvae, cards, or CPs.

💡 You must have an ant ON the collection site to harvest from it. Ants in the outskirts of a hex don't collect anything.

Step 2: Feed Ants

Count all your ants on the board (not larvae). Feed them:

FormulaBaseWith Abdomen Evolutions
1 food feeds…4 ants4 + 1 per abdomen card in stack

Unfed ants:

  1. Each unfed ant eats 1 larva from your chamber.
  2. If no larvae remain, unfed ants are destroyed (returned to supply).
⚠️ Feeding is mandatory. Over-expanding without food production will devastate your colony. Plan your food economy!

Step 3: Royal Decree

Each player chooses one:

  • 2 food, OR
  • 5 larvae
⚠️ No Royal Decree in the final round (Round 4). In the Long Game variant, no Royal Decree in Rounds 3–4.

Step 4: Slumber

  1. Score 1 CP for each Inner Meadow hex you control (hexes adjacent to the Great Tunnel).
  2. Score all active Colony Goals that meet their conditions.
  3. Advance the round marker.
💡 Inner Meadow hexes are the most valuable territory — they score every single round during Slumber. Prioritize controlling them.

Evolution Body Segments

SegmentActive AbilityCaste Bonus (per card)
🧠 HeadUnique combat/control ability (topmost only)+1 Army Strength per battle
🦵 ThoraxUnique movement/explore ability (topmost only)+1 March step
🍯 AbdomenUnique economy/harvest ability (topmost only)+1 ant fed per food

Stacking Rules

  • New evolutions stack on top of existing ones in the same segment.
  • Only the topmost card's ability is active.
  • Caste bonuses are cumulative — count ALL cards in the stack, not just the top one.
  • Example: 3 Head evolutions = topmost ability active + +3 Army Strength in every battle.

Complete Ant Body

When you have at least 1 evolution in all three segments (Head + Thorax + Abdomen), you immediately score 3 CP. This is a one-time bonus per body completion.

💡 Completing your ant body early gives you 3 CP plus ongoing caste bonuses in all three categories. It's a strong opening strategy.

Cards as Ferocity

Every card has a ferocity value (top-left corner). During battle, you may secretly discard a card to add its ferocity to your Army Strength. This creates tension: play cards for abilities or save them for combat?

🗺️ Hex & Map Rules

Hex Anatomy

Each hex tile may contain:

  • Collection sites — spaces that produce resources (food, larvae, cards, or CPs) during Harvest.
  • Outskirts — the area outside collection sites. Ants here don't collect resources.
  • Tunnel connections — edges that connect to adjacent hexes for movement.
  • Special icons — centipedes, wormholes, or unique effects.

Inhabiting & Controlling

  • Place an ant ON a collection site to inhabit it → collect its resource during Harvest.
  • The player inhabiting a site is the controller/defender in battles.
  • Multiple players can inhabit different sites on the same hex (shared hex).

Contested vs. Shared

StatusConditionBattle?
Contested2+ players present AND at least 1 player has ants in outskirtsYes — Soldier Phase
Shared2+ players inhabiting sites, no ants in outskirtsNo battle
ControlledOnly 1 player presentNo battle
⚠️ A hex becomes contested when there are more ants than collection sites AND 2+ players are present. Excess ants spill into the outskirts.

The Great Tunnel

  • Central hex where all players start.
  • Never contested — no battles ever occur here.
  • All hexes connect to it. It's the hub of the tunnel network.

Inner Meadow

Hexes adjacent to the Great Tunnel. These score 1 CP each during every Slumber phase. The most valuable real estate on the board.

Wormholes

  • Some hexes contain wormhole tokens.
  • Ants can move between any two wormhole hexes in a single step — they don't need to be adjacent.
  • Max 1 wormhole per hex.

Centipede Tokens

  • Some hexes spawn centipede tokens when explored.
  • Centipedes block collection sites and trigger battles.
  • Defeating a centipede: gain 1 food + 1 CP, remove the token.
🎯 Colony Goals

How Colony Goals Work

  • Played as an action during the Worker Phase (costs larvae/ants, not food).
  • Maximum 2 Colony Goals active at a time per player.
  • Scored during each Slumber — if you meet the condition, you gain the CP.
  • Goals are ongoing — they score every round you qualify, not just once.

⚠️ You can never have more than 2 Colony Goals active. If you want a new one, you must first discard an existing goal.
🧑 Solo Mode — Rise of the Centipedes

A solitaire (or cooperative) mode where you battle an invading Centipede Horde overrunning the Meadow. You must defend the Great Tunnel.

Setup Changes

  • Use the reverse side of the Great Tunnel.
  • Remove centipede-icon cards → shuffle into a separate Centipede Deck.
  • Start with 1 random starting hex with 3 ants, 3 larvae, 2 food, 4 cards.

Centipede Actions

After each of your turns in the Worker Phase, reveal 1 card from the Centipede Deck:

Card Type RevealedCentipede Action
🧬 EvolutionInvade: Place a centipede on the highest-numbered hex without one. If all hexes full → centipede enters Great Tunnel.
🎴 EventAdvance: Centipede on lowest-numbered hex jumps toward lower numbers (# of jumps = card value). If no lower hex → enters Great Tunnel.
🎯 Colony GoalRampage: Centipede battle in EVERY hex with both ants and a centipede. Each centipede gets +1 Army Strength during Rampage.
⚠️ Whenever a centipede enters the Great Tunnel, the Horde gains CP equal to the number of players. Centipedes in the Great Tunnel cannot be battled and score every Slumber.

Centipede Sentry

If an Event card is revealed but no centipedes exist to move: explore a new hex anywhere in the Meadow and place a centipede on it. If that hex has a centipede icon, reveal another centipede card.

Combat Differences

  • After taking a March action, you may initiate a centipede battle in one hex.
  • Centipedes draw from the Centipede Deck for Army Strength.

Scoring & Winning

  • Centipede Horde gains CP: each Slumber (per centipede in Meadow + Great Tunnel), each battle won, each Great Tunnel entry.
  • No end-of-game bonuses for most food/larvae/cards.
  • If the Horde's CP exceeds yours at game end → you lose.

Difficulty Levels

EasyStart with 3 additional larvae in your chamber
NormalStandard solo setup (3 ants, 3 larvae, 2 food, 4 cards)
HardStart with only 2 cards in hand (instead of 4)

Worker Phase End (Solo)

After you rest, the Centipede reveals 1 final card, then the Worker Phase ends.

👥 2-Player Variant

Setup Changes

  • Use the reverse side of the Great Tunnel.
  • Each player takes 2 random unused starting hexes → choose 1 to place connected to the Great Tunnel.
  • Start with 3 ants in the placed hex, 3 larvae, 2 food, 2 cards.

Resting (2-Player)

After one player rests, the other player gets one final turn (any action, including rest). That player keeps the Active Player Marker and becomes the Starting Player next round.

Inner Meadow Scoring

Inner Meadow hexes must be connected by tunnels to the Great Tunnel to score during Slumber. (In 3-5 player games, adjacency alone is sufficient.)

Colony Goals

"Neighbor" goals compare only to the other player (since you each have only 1 neighbor).

💡 The 2-player game is tighter and more aggressive. With fewer players competing for hexes, direct conflict is more common. Plan your military strength accordingly.
👥 5-Player Variant

Setup

Include all 8 starting hex tiles.

Reactions

Only your immediate neighbors (players to your left and right) may take Reactions to your actions. Card effects that target "all players" still affect everyone.

Resting

Worker Phase continues until 3 players rest (instead of 2). The 3rd player to rest becomes the next Starting Player.

📏 Long Game & Beyond the Meadow

Long Game Variant

  • Play 5 rounds instead of 4.
  • No Royal Decree in Rounds 3 and 4 (only Rounds 1, 2, and 5 have it — wait, Round 5 is the final round so no Royal Decree there either).
⚠️ In the Long Game, Royal Decree is skipped in Rounds 3–4, and also skipped in Round 5 (final round). So you only get Royal Decree in Rounds 1 and 2. Plan your economy carefully!

Beyond the Meadow Variant

Special hex tiles numbered #21–25 with unique effects.

  • Before the game, shuffle these special hexes and randomly add 2–3 into the hex tile stack.
  • These hexes have powerful or unusual effects that change the game dynamics.
  • Can be combined with any other variant.
💡 Beyond the Meadow adds variety and surprises. Great for experienced players who want more unpredictability.
