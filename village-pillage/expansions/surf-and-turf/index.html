🌊 Village Pillage: Surf & Turf

Expansion · 2–5 players · Jellybean Games / Blue Beard Entertainment (2020)

📦 What's New

Surf & Turf is two mini-expansions in one pack. You can add either or both to the base game.

HalfAddsKey Mechanic
SURF Freebooter cards — a new card type Chain-buying: purchase a Freebooter, then spend 1 more turnip to buy another card
TURF New villager cards with animal companions; table-wide effect cards Provoke — the opposite of Exhaust

Freebooters SURF — A brand-new card type that sails into your hand faster than any other card. When you buy a Freebooter from the market, you may immediately spend 1 additional turnip to buy another card (which can be another Freebooter, chaining further). However, Freebooters abandon ship at the first sign of trouble — they leave your hand when they lose a matchup.

Provoke TURF — The opposite of Exhaust. When a card is Provoked, you must play it next turn. While Exhaust forces a card out of your options, Provoke forces a card into your plans.

Table-Wide Effects TURF — Some Turf villagers have abilities that affect every player at the table, not just your two neighbors. This works at any player count.

Animal Pals TURF — New villager cards come with animal companions that provide bonus effects or modify how the card interacts in matchups.

🔀 Rule Changes

Freebooter Chain-Buying SURF

  1. During the Merchant phase, buy a Freebooter from the market as normal (pay its turnip cost).
  2. After buying the Freebooter, you may immediately spend 1 additional turnip to buy any other card from the market.
  3. If that second card is also a Freebooter, you may chain again — spend 1 more turnip for yet another card.
  4. The chain continues as long as you keep buying Freebooters and have turnips to spend.
💡 Tip: Chain-buying lets you stock up fast, but each extra purchase still costs 1 turnip on top of the card's price. Budget carefully.

Abandon Ship SURF

  • When a Freebooter loses a matchup (i.e. the opponent's card beats it), the Freebooter is discarded from your hand — it abandons ship.
  • You do not get it back. It returns to the market supply.
⚠️ Key difference: Losing a Freebooter is permanent removal from your hand, not just an Exhaust. Plan around this fragility.

Provoke vs Exhaust TURF

MechanicEffectWhen It Triggers
Exhaust Card cannot be played next turn After certain matchup results (base game)
Provoke Card must be played next turn Triggered by specific Turf villager abilities
  • If a card is both Provoked and Exhausted at the same time, Provoke wins — you must play it.
  • A Provoked card must be assigned to one of your two neighbors (your choice which side).

Table-Wide Effects TURF

  • Some Turf cards have abilities that resolve against every player, not just the neighbor you played the card against.
  • This is especially impactful in 2-player games where you normally only interact with one opponent on each side.
⚙️ Setup Changes

You can add Surf, Turf, or both to the base game. Setup is the same as the base game with these additions:

Adding Surf:

  1. Shuffle the Freebooter cards into the market supply alongside the base game market cards.
  2. Freebooters are available for purchase during the Merchant phase like any other market card.

Adding Turf:

  1. Add the new Turf villager cards (with animal pals) to the market supply.
  2. All Turf cards are purchased the same way as base game cards — via the Merchant.

Adding Both:

  • Simply add all Surf and Turf cards to the market. No other changes to setup.
  • Player count, starting hands, and relic victory condition remain unchanged.
💡 First time? Try adding just one half first to learn the new mechanics, then combine both once everyone is comfortable.
🏆 Scoring Changes

The victory condition is unchanged — first player to collect 3 Relics wins.

Surf & Turf does not add new relics or alter relic costs. The expansion adds new strategic paths to earn turnips and control the table, but the end goal stays the same.

⚠️ Gotchas & Common Mistakes
Freebooters leave permanently. When a Freebooter loses a matchup, it goes back to the market — it's not just Exhausted. You lose the card entirely.
Chain-buying costs extra. The 1-turnip chain cost is in addition to the purchased card's normal price. Buying a Freebooter (cost X) then chaining into another card (cost Y) costs X + 1 + Y total.
Provoke overrides Exhaust. If a card is somehow both Provoked and Exhausted, you must play it. Don't accidentally skip it thinking Exhaust takes priority.
Provoked card — you choose the side. You must play the Provoked card, but you still decide which neighbor to play it against. Your opponent doesn't choose for you.
Table-wide ≠ both sides. Table-wide effects hit all players, including those not adjacent to you. In a 3+ player game, this can affect players you didn't play a card against this turn.
💡 Don't over-invest in Freebooters. They're fast to acquire but fragile. A hand full of Freebooters can collapse if opponents play strong counters. Balance them with sturdy base-game cards.
