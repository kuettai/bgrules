Dice Mechanics

At the start of each day, roll all your tackle dice and place them in your fresh pool. Spend dice whose total ≥ a fish's catch difficulty to reel it in. Higher-tier dice (bought at port) have higher face values. Dinks and gear can reduce difficulty or add virtual dice.

💡 A life preserver token rotates between players, granting a once-per-round discount on one catch.

Regret Penalty

⚠️ After scoring, the player with the highest total regret value (sum of hidden card values) must discard their highest-scoring mounted fish. This can cost up to 27 points! Ties broken by turn order.

The number of regret cards is public, but their values (0–3 each) are hidden until end-game reveal.

Tiebreaker

If scores are tied, the player with fewer regret cards wins. If still tied, the player who passed first on the final day wins.

💡 Mounted fish are your biggest point source. Prioritize filling all 3 mount slots — especially the ×3 "prize catch" slot.
⚙️ Quick Setup
  1. Ocean Board: Place the 3×3 grid of face-down fish cards — 3 shoals per depth (I, II, III). Card backs show small/medium/large fish silhouettes.
  2. Port Board: Set up the market — deal rod, reel, and supply cards into their respective shops.
  3. Ocean Madness Board: Place each player's cube at the starting position (0 regrets).
  4. Player Boards: Each player takes a double-sided player board (both sides functionally identical), 3 starting tackle dice, and starting fishbucks.
  5. Decks: Shuffle Depth I, II, III fish decks separately. Shuffle the Dink deck, Regret deck, and gear decks (rods, reels, supplies).
  6. Life Preserver: Give to the starting player. Metal fish coin nearby for resolving card effects.
  7. Day Tracker: Set to Monday.
💡 Card backs at each depth hint at fish size (small/medium/large). Use this to gauge risk before flipping!
⚠️ Gotchas & Common Mistakes

Official Errata

⚠️ Depth I Fair:Foul ratio is 2:1, not 3:1 as printed in some rulebooks.
⚠️ Fish size corrections: Box Jellyfish = small, Corpse = large, Sockeye Salmon = small.
⚠️ Cycle market icon on reference cards should be ignored.

Frequently Missed Rules

  • Simultaneous choice: Sea/Port decisions are revealed at the same time — no peeking at others' choices first.
  • Locked in: Once you choose Sea or Port, you stay there for the entire day (lifeboat is the only exception, and it costs a regret).
  • Passing draws Dinks: When you pass, you draw a Dink. If you've already passed and others are still acting, you keep drawing additional Dinks each time it comes back to you.
  • Regret count is public, value is secret: Everyone can see how many regret cards you have, but not what's on them (values 0–3).
  • Selling foul fish = regret: Selling a foul fish at port earns you a regret card. Don't forget!
  • Mounted fish are locked: Once mounted, a fish can't be removed or swapped (unless a card specifically breaks this rule).
  • Regret deck empty? When the regret deck runs out, additional regrets must be stolen from other players — very swingy late-game!
  • Eating fish: Discarding a fish for its ability means you lose its point value permanently.
🌊 Fishing at Sea (Detailed)

Depth Mechanics

The ocean board has a 3×3 grid of face-down fish card piles. Each row corresponds to a depth level:

DepthFish SizeDifficultyFair:Foul RatioRisk/Reward
I ShallowsMostly smallLow2:1Safe, low value
II Mid-watersMixedMedium~1:1Balanced
III The DeepMostly largeHighMore foulHigh risk, high reward

You can spend a die to move to a deeper depth during your turn. Card backs show silhouettes indicating small, medium, or large fish — use this to gauge risk before flipping.

Catch Resolution

  1. Choose a shoal at your current depth (one of 3 piles).
  2. Flip the top card. Resolve any "on reveal" effects (e.g., Whiptail Stingray lets you reroll a die; some fish affect all players).
  3. Decide to catch or not. Spend dice whose total ≥ the fish's catch difficulty. Gear, dinks, and abilities can reduce difficulty.
  4. If you can't/won't catch: Draw a Dink card as consolation.
  5. If caught: Add to your hand. Resolve any "on catch" effects. Foul fish may trigger regret gains.

Fair vs Foul Fish

Fair Normal real-world fish (frilled shark, sockeye salmon, etc.). Worth more at low madness.

Foul Eldritch horrors, body parts, mutants (psychic interloper, corpse, etc.). Worth more at high madness. Selling foul fish at port earns a regret.

Dinks

Tiny consolation fish drawn when you fail a catch or pass. They provide small bonuses — e.g., Shrimp reduces a fish's difficulty by 1. You keep drawing Dinks while passed if others are still acting.

💡 Some fish can be "eaten" (discarded from hand) to activate a special ability. Powerful, but you lose the fish's point value permanently.
💡 Interaction cards exist — e.g., the Tricky Squid gets gifted to another player when revealed. Event cards in the fish decks can shake things up for everyone.

Market Shops

The port has separate shops for rods, reels, and supplies. Each has a face-up display. When you buy, you spend fishbucks and may draw multiple cards, keeping a subset.

⚠️ Selling foul fish earns you a regret card! Factor this into your sell decisions.
💡 Port days are precious — you're giving up a fishing day. Plan port trips to maximize gear purchases and mount your best catches.
💡 The value of unsold fish in your hand counts toward your final score. Don't sell everything!
💀 Regret & Madness System

How Regret Is Gained

  • Catching certain foul fish or deep-sea horrors
  • Selling foul fish at port
  • Specific card effects and events
  • Using the emergency lifeboat (to switch from Sea to Port mid-round)

Regret cards have a hidden value (0–3) and flavor text ranging from darkly funny ("Over-salted my breakfast" — 0) to sinister ("Partook of human flesh" — 3).

Ocean Madness Track

Each player has a cube on the shared Ocean Madness board. Gaining a regret card moves your cube down one level; discarding one moves it back up.

Strategic Tension

More regrets = more dice = catch bigger fish, BUT foul fish become more valuable while fair fish lose value. And the player with the highest total regret value at end-game loses their best mounted fish.

Stay Sane → Fair fish worth more → Fewer dice → Safe but limited
Embrace Madness → Foul fish worth more → More dice → Powerful but risky

End-Game Regret Penalty

⚠️ The player with the highest total regret value must discard their highest-scoring mounted fish. This can swing the game by up to 27 points. Ties broken by turn order.

Number of regret cards is always public. Values are hidden until the final reveal.

💡 When the regret deck runs out, additional regrets are stolen from other players — making late-game turns extremely swingy. Track the deck size!
🎣 Gear Reference

Gear is purchased at port using fishbucks. Spend more to draw more cards and keep fewer (e.g., spend $5, draw 5, keep 2).

Rods 🎣

Ongoing passive abilities that improve your fishing. Rods stay equipped permanently once purchased.

  • Reduce catch difficulty at certain depths
  • Grant bonus effects when catching specific fish types
  • Allow rerolls or dice manipulation

Reels 🔄

Ongoing abilities that improve your catches and hand management.

  • Increase the value of caught fish
  • Allow catching multiple fish per action
  • Provide discounts on mounting or selling

Supplies 📦

One-time-use cards with powerful effects. Discarded after use.

  • Instant dice bonuses or rerolls
  • Remove or transfer regret cards
  • Peek at face-down fish before committing
  • Various utility effects (energy recovery, market manipulation)
💡 Rods and reels are investments — buy them early to benefit across multiple days. Supplies are tactical — save them for critical moments.
🖼️ Mounting & Fish Scoring

Mount Slots

Each player board has 3 mount slots with increasing multipliers:

SlotMultiplierNotes
Slot 1×1No bonus — just locks the fish in for scoring
Slot 2×2Doubles the fish's value
Prize Catch×3Triples the fish's value — save your best fish for this!

Mounting Rules

  • Mounting is a port action — costs a die to perform
  • Once mounted, a fish is locked in and cannot be removed or swapped (unless a card specifically overrides this)
  • Mounted fish are scored at end-game with their multiplier applied
  • The regret penalty forces the most regretful player to discard their highest-scoring mounted fish (value × multiplier)

Fish Value Modifiers

A fish's effective value depends on your position on the Ocean Madness track:

  • At low madness: Fair fish get up to +2, Foul fish get −2
  • At high madness: Foul fish get up to +2, Fair fish get −2
⚠️ Mount your ×3 fish carefully — if you end up with the most regret, that's the fish most likely to be discarded (highest score = value × multiplier).
💡 Fish still in your hand at end-game score at face value (no multiplier). Mounting is almost always better if you have the port time.
🧑 Solo Mode

Overview

The solo/co-op mode casts you as an ichthyologist on a mission to catch and catalog every fish in the sea. It plays as a campaign-lite experience over multiple sessions.

Setup Differences

  • Use the provided survey/catalog sheet (paper pad) to track discoveries
  • Specific mission objectives replace competitive scoring
  • No Ocean Madness competition — regret still matters but penalty works differently
  • Standard 6-day structure still applies

Campaign Goals

  • Catch and document every unique fish species across all 3 depths
  • Record fish attributes on the catalog sheet
  • Complete specific mission objectives each session
  • Upgrade gear across sessions to reach deeper, rarer catches
💡 The solo mode is described as a cozy "gotta catch 'em all" experience — less competitive tension, more exploration and collection.
