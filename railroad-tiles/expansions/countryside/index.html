🐄 Railroad Tiles: Countryside

Expansion · 1–4 players · 30 min · Horrible Guild / Cranio Creations

"Build pastures, herd animals, avoid overcrowding."

📦 What's New

The Countryside expansion adds barns, fences, animal meeples, and village tiles to Railroad Tiles. Build pastures alongside your routes and populate them with herds of pigs, cows, and sheep.

🐑 Animal Meeples

Cute wooden pigs, cows, and sheep drawn randomly from a cloth bag — not printed on tiles. Each column in the display gets one random animal during the draft.

🏚️ Barn / Farm Tiles

New countryside tiles feature barns (farms). Farms act as a scoring multiplier — each animal in a pasture scores 1 point per farm in that same pasture.

🧱 Fences & Pastures

Countryside tiles have fences that divide fields. Connected fields form pastures, separated by highways, railways, towns, and fences.

🏘️ Village Tiles

Villages can join cities like town tiles, but unlike towns they do not break pasture connections — key for building large pastures.

🎯 New Objective Tiles

Expansion-specific objectives, e.g. Vineyard (5+ connected field tiles with no animals) or Hills (at least 3 animals adjacent).

💡 This is considered one of the trickiest expansions despite its cute, family-friendly appearance. Managing pasture boundaries is deceptively hard.
🔀 Rule Changes
  • Animals go in fields, not on routes. Unlike cars, trains, and travelers, animal pawns are placed in the open field areas surrounding routes.
  • Animals are tied to columns. When you draft a column of tiles, you also get the animal assigned to that column. You don't choose your animal freely.
  • Placement restriction: Animals must be placed on one of the tiles you are placing this round — you cannot place them on previously placed tiles.
  • Pasture definition: Fields connected to each other form a "pasture." Pastures are separated by highways, railways, towns, and fences on countryside tiles.
  • Duplicate penalty: If a pasture contains more than one animal of the same type (e.g. two cows), each duplicate beyond the first is removed and you lose 1 point per removed animal.
  • Villages ≠ Towns for pastures: Village tiles can form cities with town tiles, but they do not separate pastures (towns do).
⚠️ Duplicate animals are removed and cost you a point each. It's a double penalty — you lose the animal and the point.
⚙️ Setup Changes
  1. Set up the base game as normal (station board, route tiles in bag, placement tokens, etc.).
  2. Add the Countryside tiles (with barns and fences) to the cloth bag and shuffle together with base route tiles.
  3. Place all animal meeples (pigs, cows, sheep) into a separate cloth bag or supply.
  4. During each round's draft: after drawing tiles into columns, draw one random animal from the bag and place it on each column in the display.
  5. Shuffle the Countryside objective tiles into the objective supply.
💡 Animals are assigned to columns randomly — you pick your animal when you pick your column. Plan your draft around which animal you need, not just which tiles.
🏆 Scoring Changes

At end of game, score each pasture individually:

  • Per pasture: Each animal in the pasture scores 1 point × number of farms in that pasture.
  • Duplicate check: Before scoring, if a pasture has more than one animal of the same type, remove all duplicates beyond the first of each type. Lose 1 point per removed animal.
  • Ideal pasture: One of each animal type (cow, pig, sheep) + many farms = maximum points.
  • Objective tiles: Score their specific conditions as printed (e.g. Vineyard: 5+ connected fields with no animals; Hills: 3+ adjacent animals).
💡 A pasture with 3 farms and 3 unique animals scores 9 points (3 animals × 3 farms). Farms are the multiplier — prioritize them.
⚠️ Gotchas / Common Mistakes

❌ Placing animals on old tiles

Animals must go on a tile you're placing this round. You can't retroactively add them to tiles from previous rounds.

❌ Accidentally merging pastures

Placing a tile that connects two pastures can create duplicates. Always check what animals are already in adjacent pastures before placing.

❌ Confusing villages and towns

Towns break pastures; villages do not. A village connects to cities but keeps fields joined — this is a critical distinction.

❌ Ignoring the column animal

The animal is tied to the column you draft. Don't pick a column just for tiles — check which animal comes with it.

❌ Forgetting the double penalty

Duplicate animals are removed and cost −1 point each. It's not just "no score" — it's active point loss.

❌ Empty farms score nothing

Farms multiply animal count. A pasture with 5 farms but 0 animals scores 0. You need both farms and animals.

⚠️ Despite the cute animal meeples, this is considered one of the hardest Railroad Tiles expansions. Pasture management requires careful spatial planning every round.
