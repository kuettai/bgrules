🏜️ Railroad Tiles: Desert Expansion

Expansion · 1–4 players · 30–45 min · Requires Railroad Tiles base game

📦 What's New
  • Desert tiles — new tile type with sandy terrain that must form a single connected desert area
  • Camel meeples — replace Train pawns on desert-specific pin points; placed using Train Placement tokens
  • Desert Trails — special routes on desert tiles that connect to both Highways and Railways
  • Desert Stations — placed at the junction where a Desert Trail meets a Highway or Railway
  • 4 Desert Attractions — Oasis, Pyramid, Sphinx, and Obelisk for set-collection scoring
  • New Objectives — desert-themed objective cards (e.g. Desert Market, Great Oasis)
💡 This expansion adds a set-collection layer on top of the base tile-placement game. Think of it as building a tourist desert surrounding your rail network.
🔀 Rule Changes

Desert Tile Placement

  • The first Desert tile of the game can be placed freely (no adjacency restriction)
  • All subsequent tiles (regular or Desert) must be placed so at least 1 side connects to a tile of the same kind
  • This means all desert tiles must stay connected as one contiguous desert area

Camels

  • Desert tiles have Camel pin points instead of Train pin points (Car and Traveler pins still appear normally)
  • Spend a Train Placement token to place a Camel meeple on a Camel pin point
  • When placed, score 1 point per Desert Station the Camel connects to

Desert Trails & Stations

  • Desert Trails are special routes printed on desert tiles
  • They can connect to both Highways and Railways
  • When a Desert Trail joins a Highway or Railway, place a Desert Station token on the junction

Desert Attractions

  • 4 types: Oasis, Pyramid, Sphinx, Obelisk
  • Placed on desert tiles; must be connected to at least 1 Desert Station to score
⚠️ Desert tiles lack Train pin points entirely — you cannot place Train pawns on desert tiles, only Camels.
⚙️ Setup Changes
  1. Shuffle the Desert tiles into the regular tile supply (or keep separate per rulebook preference)
  2. Place the Camel meeples near the play area as a shared supply
  3. Place the Desert Station tokens nearby
  4. Shuffle in the Desert Objective cards if using objectives
💡 All base game components remain — desert components are purely additive.
🏆 Scoring Changes

All base game scoring still applies. The Desert expansion adds:

Camel Scoring (Immediate)

  • When you place a Camel: score 1 point per Desert Station it connects to

Attraction Set Scoring (End of Game)

Score points for sets of different Desert Attractions connected to at least 1 Desert Station:

Different AttractionsPoints
1 type1
2 types3
3 types6
4 types (full set)10
⚠️ Each Attraction can only count toward one set. Attractions in a set don't need to connect to the same Desert Station or to each other — they just each need at least 1 Desert Station connection.

Example Objectives

  • Desert Market — must be part of a route with at least 5 connected Desert Trail segments
  • Great Oasis — must be surrounded by at least 4 Desert tiles; also counts as an Oasis Attraction
⚠️ Gotchas / Common Mistakes
  • Same-kind adjacency is mandatory after the first desert tile — regular tiles must also touch regular tiles, desert tiles must touch desert tiles. Plan your layout carefully
  • Camels use Train Placement tokens — you're spending the same resource, so every Camel placed is a Train you didn't place
  • No Trains on desert tiles — desert tiles have Camel pin points only, never Train pin points
  • Desert Stations are placed at junctions, not on tiles — they mark where a Desert Trail meets a Highway or Railway
  • Attractions must connect to a Desert Station to score — an isolated Attraction surrounded by desert but with no Station connection scores nothing
  • One Attraction per set — if you have 2 Pyramids, the second one starts a new (smaller) set
💡 Strategy tip: Prioritize building Desert Stations early — they're the key multiplier for both Camel scoring and Attraction set scoring. A full 4-type set is worth 10 points, which is significant.
